Privacy Policy

1. Introduction

We are committed to protecting your personal information which we collect, process, and disclose on behalf of Kwara State Government of Nigeria, through the Kwara State Resident Registration Agency, a body corporate duly established by the Kwara State House of Assembly. We are committed to ensuring that data collected is handled in a safe and responsible manner. This Privacy Policy outlines the types of personal information we collect on our portal/system, how we protect that information, and your rights regarding your personal information, in accordance with applicable data protection laws and regulations. This privacy policy describes how the Kwara State Resident Registration Agency (“KWASRRA/the Agency”) collects, uses, and discloses your personal information. This privacy policy may be amended from time to time.

2. Purpose

Kwara State (“the State”) is one of the 36 (Thirty-six) States and a federating unit of the Federal Republic of Nigeria duly established by the Constitution of the Federal Republic of Nigeria, 1999 (as amended) (“the Constitution”). The State is bound by the provisions of Chapter 2 of the Constitution (Fundamental Objectives and Directive Principles of State Policy) which imposes economic, and socio-political obligations on the federating states of Nigeria.

Thus, Kwara State Government (“KWSG”) is committed to the provision of socio-economic services such as education, sanitation, healthcare, etc to the residents of the State particularly those in disadvantaged and rural areas. The KWSG is also determined to escalate its role in addressing poverty, social injustice, inequality, etc through dedicated policies and programs. To actualize these objectives, the KWSG established the Agency which has identified the need to collect, and process the personal information of the residents of the State as one of the strategies to be deployed to carry out the mandate of the KWSG as articulated above, as this would enable the KWSG identify the areas of greatest need and allocate resources accordingly, in a more effective and optimal manner.

3. Expected Outcomes

The collection of personal information of the residents of the State aims to achieve several key outcomes which will enable the State to achieve its obligations to the residents of Kwara State and these include:

3.1 Economic Planning and Development: Support for economic planning and development initiatives through data-driven insights, promoting sustainable growth and development.

3.2 Efficient Resource Allocation: Better allocation of resources and services based on accurate demographic and geographic data, which ensures that community needs are met effectively.

3.3 Informed Policy Making: Development of evidence-based policies and programs that address the specific needs and challenges of the residents of the State.

3.4 Security and Law Enforcement: Strengthened security and law enforcement efforts by maintaining accurate records, facilitating investigations, and ensuring public safety.

3.5 Enhanced Public Services: Improved delivery of public services such as education, social services, and public safety through accurate and up-to-date information.

3.6 Improved Public Health:Enhanced ability to monitor and respond to health crises, manage epidemics, pandemics such as Covid-19, and implement effective public health interventions.

3.7 Emergency Preparedness and Response: Enhanced capacity to respond to emergencies and disasters, providing timely assistance and support to affected individuals.

3.8 Compliance with Legal Requirements: Fulfilment of legal obligations and regulatory requirements, ensuring that government operations are transparent and accountable.

3.9 Civic Engagement: Better management of civic activities such as elections, enabling fair and efficient electoral processes and enhancing citizens’ participation.

3.10 Social Equity: Promotion of social equity by identifying and addressing disparities in access to services and resources among different population groups.

By achieving these outcomes, the State can improve the overall quality of life for its citizens, ensure equitable access to resources and services, and enhance the effectiveness and efficiency of its operations.

4. Definitions and Interpretations

In this policy, the following terms have the meanings set against them:

4.1 Anonymization: means the process of protecting private or sensitive data by erasing or encrypting identifiers that connect the owner of the personal information to data stored in regard to that person.

4.2 Biometrics: means biological information about a person to create a detailed record of their personal characteristics.

4.3 Data: means information, facts, and figures collected for reference, analysis, and processing.

4.4 Data Controller: means a natural or legal person, public authority, agency, or any other body, which alone or jointly with others, determines the purposes and means of the processing of personal data. For the purposes of this policy, the Kwara State Resident Registration Agency is the Data Controller.

4.5 Data Processor: means a natural or legal person, public authority, agency, or other body which processes personal data on behalf of the controller. Proximaforte Limited is our Data Processor.

4.6 Data Subject: means an individual whose personal data is collected, held, or processed. In the context of data protection laws like the Nigerian Data Protection Act 2023, the data subject is the person to whom the personal information collected pertains.

4.7 Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 ("NDPA/the Act"): Enhanced ability to monitor and respond to health crises, manage epidemics, pandemics such as Covid-19, and implement effective public health interventions.

4.8 Personal Information: means any data that can identify an individual, either directly or indirectly. This includes details such as name, address, email address, phone number, date of birth, passport number, financial information, medical records, biometric data, IP address.

4.9 Processing: means any operation or set of operations performed on personal data, whether or not by automated means, such as the collection, recording, organization, storage, adaptation or alteration, retrieval, gathering, use, disclosure by transmission, dissemination or otherwise making information available, alignment or combination, blocking, erasure or destruction.

4.10 Profiling: means the use of data to evaluate certain aspects related to a Data Subject. The purpose is to predict the Data Subject's behavior and take decisions regarding him or her.

4.11 Sensitive Personal Information: means genetic and biometric personal information of an individual for the purpose of uniquely identifying a person’s race or ethnic origin, religious or similar beliefs, such as those reflecting conscience or philosophy, health status, political opinions or affiliations, and trade union membership.

4.12 Legal Framework: Sharing personal information is governed by relevant data protection laws and regulations, such as:
  • Section 37 of the Constitution of the Federal Republic of Nigeria, 1999 which guarantees the right to private and family life;
  • The Nigeria Data Protection Act (NDPA) 2023 (‘the Act’), provides a legal framework for the protection of Personal Information and establishes the Nigeria Data Protection Commission (“the Commission”) as its supervisory body.
  • Other data protection laws and regulations, which dictate how data can be shared and the safeguards required.
These laws and regulations ensure that Personal Information is shared in a manner that respects privacy rights, complies with legal obligations, and serves the public interest.

5. Personal Information

You may be required to provide certain Personal Information that can be used to contact or identify you. Personal Information may include, but is not limited to:

5.1 Contact details: email address, name, phone number, address, state of origin, local government area, city/town.

5.2 Demographic Information: date of birth, age, occupation, profession.

5.3 Sensitive Personal Information: gender, biometric data, religious or similar beliefs.

5.4 Financial Information: income and salary information, tax identification numbers, insurance policy information, loan and debt information and other similar information, which may be required to open a bank account for you with a Central Bank of Nigeria-licensed bank in order to enable you access any financial support you may be eligible for from the KWSG and/or other third-party financial institutions or support groups.

5.5 Other information: National Identification Number (NIN), image of a government-issued identity document, international passport, driving license, permanent voter card, photograph etc.

6. How Personal Information Collected is Shared

Personal Information collected is shared under specific conditions as approved by the Data Controller to ensure it is used responsibly and in accordance with legal and ethical standards. Specifically, your Personal Information may be shared in the following circumstances:

6.1 With Government Agencies:

  • Interagency Collaboration: Personal Information may be shared with other government departments or agencies to facilitate public services, policy implementation, and law enforcement.
  • Public Health Management: Data may be shared with health departments to track and manage public health issues, such as during epidemics and pandemics.

6.2 With Third-Party Service Providers:

  • Service Delivery: Third-party providers may process data on behalf of the State to deliver services, such as IT support, personal data storage, and healthcare services.
  • Data Analysis: Specialized firms may be engaged to analyze personal data to improve public services and inform policy decisions.

6.3 For Legal Compliance:

  • Law Enforcement: Personal Information may be shared with law enforcement agencies to comply with legal investigations, court orders, and subpoenas.
  • Regulatory Requirements: Personal data sharing may occur to comply with regulatory obligations and audits.

6.4 With Researchers and Academics:

  • Public Health and Social Research: Personal data may be provided to researchers and academic institutions for studies that aim to improve public health, social services, and policy outcomes.

6.5 Anonymized Data:

  • Whenever possible, personal data is anonymized or aggregated to protect the privacy of an individual.

6.6 In Emergency Situations:

  • Disaster Response: Personal Information may be shared to coordinate emergency response efforts and provide timely assistance to those affected by any form of disaster.
  • Public Safety: Sharing data to ensure public safety and manage crises, such as during a pandemic or natural disaster.

6.7 With Consent of Data Subject:

  • Personal information may be shared with other entities if the Data Subject has given explicit consent for such sharing.

6.8 Public Interest and Statistical Purposes:

  • Statistical Analysis: Personal data may be used for statistical purposes to inform government planning and policy-making.
  • Public Reports: Aggregated and anonymized personal data may be published in public reports to provide transparency and inform citizens.
7. Prohibited Use

Personal information may be shared with other entities if the Data Subject has given explicit consent for such sharing. However, there are prohibited uses where sharing may not be permitted:

7.1 Necessary for entering into or for the performance of a contract between the Data Subject and a Data Controller;

7.2 Authorized by a written law, which establishes suitable measures to safeguard fundamental rights and freedoms;

7.3 In the interest of the Data Subject; or

7.4 Authorized by the consent of the Data Subject.

8. Safeguards and Protections

To ensure Personal Information is shared responsibly, we implement several safeguards, which include the following:

8.1 Data Protection Agreements: Ensuring third parties comply with data protection standards through contractual agreements.

8.2 Encryption and Security Measures: Utilizing encryption and other security measures to protect data during transmission and storage.

8.3 Access Controls: Restricting access to personal data to authorized personnel only.

8.4 Audits and Monitoring: Regularly auditing data sharing practices and monitoring compliance with privacy laws, policies, and regulations.

8.5 Anonymization and Aggregation: Anonymizing personal data wherever necessary and possible, in order to minimize the risk of identifying the Data Subject.

9.Rights of Data Subjects

Data Controllers and Data Processors are obligated under the Act to implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure the security, integrity, and confidentiality of personal data. Your rights as a Data Subject under the Act are as follows:

9.1 Right to be informed: The Data Controller is to inform the Data Subject about the Processing and the purpose for Processing of personal data in a clear, concise, transparent, and intelligible manner.

9.2 Right of access: As a Data Subject, you have the right to access your personal data and obtain a copy of your personal data, and other supplementary information from the Data Processor.

9.3 Right to correction of your personal data: This right ensures that the Data Controller or Data Processor shall ensure that personal data is accurate, complete, not misleading, and where necessary, kept up to date.

9.4 Right to erasure/deletion: You have the right to have your personal data removed from our records if:
  • The personal data is no longer necessary in relation to the purposes for which it was collected or processed, or
  • The Data Controller has no other lawful basis to retain the personal data.

9.5 Right to restriction of Processing: You have the right to restrict the Processing of your personal data under certain circumstances.

9.6 Right to withdraw consent: You have the right to withdraw your consent to the Processing of your personal data at any time.

9.7 Right to object to Processing: This is an absolute right that allows you to object to the processing of your personal data, as long as the exercise of this right to object is not exempted by law.

9.8 Right to lodge a complaint with the Commission: You have the right to lodge a complaint with the Commission if you believe the Data Controller has violated your rights under the Act.

9.9 Right to Data Portability: You have the right to receive your personal data in a structured, commonly used, and machine-readable format, and to transmit this data to another Data Controller without hindrance, where technically feasible.
